# Research Proposal — Working Draft

## Title
**Dynamic Characterization and Model Calibration of Machine-Tool Spindle–Bearing Systems Considering Support and Housing Flexibility**

**Applicant:** Youngseok Cho  
**Intended degree:** Master's in Mechanical Engineering  
**Target entry:** April 2028, subject to the selected programme's official intake  
**Status:** Research concept for discussion; not a fixed commitment or a claim of completed research.

## 1. Background and motivation
The dynamic behaviour of a machine-tool spindle is influenced not only by the rotating shaft but also by bearing supports, preload-dependent stiffness, housing flexibility and the boundary conditions connecting the spindle unit to the machine structure. Simplified support assumptions can therefore affect predicted natural frequencies and frequency-response functions.

The applicant's professional context is machine-tool structural and vibration CAE, including spindle and bearing systems. Graduate study is an opportunity to strengthen the physical modeling, experimental characterization and parameter-identification methods needed to assess these coupled systems more rigorously.

## 2. Research question
How do bearing-support properties and housing flexibility influence the dynamic response of a machine-tool spindle system, and which parameters can be reliably identified or calibrated from available dynamic measurements?

Sub-questions:
1. How sensitive are system-level modal properties and FRFs to bearing stiffness and support assumptions?
2. Under what conditions does housing flexibility materially change the predicted response?
3. Which measurements and response features provide sufficient information to estimate uncertain model parameters?
4. How should model uncertainty and parameter non-uniqueness be reported?

## 3. Objectives
- Establish a clearly bounded spindle–bearing–housing dynamic model.
- Compare selected support and housing idealizations under consistent conditions.
- Quantify sensitivity of selected dynamic response quantities to uncertain parameters.
- If laboratory facilities and supervision permit, compare numerical predictions with experimental modal or frequency-response measurements.
- Develop a defensible calibration workflow with explicit uncertainty and limitations.

## 4. Proposed methodology
### WP1 — Literature review and model definition
Review spindle dynamics, rolling-bearing stiffness models, support boundary conditions, structural coupling, modal testing and model updating. Define the system boundary, coordinate conventions, operating conditions and response quantities with the supervisor.

### WP2 — Baseline numerical model
Build or adapt a model using the laboratory's available tools. Represent the shaft, bearing supports and housing at a fidelity justified by the research question. Document assumptions, material properties, joints, constraints and parameter sources. Begin with a baseline model before adding complexity.

### WP3 — Sensitivity and parameter identifiability
Vary selected parameters (for example, bearing-support stiffness, preload-related parameters where data are available, and housing/interface stiffness). Examine changes in natural frequencies and complex FRFs. Use sensitivity and identifiability analysis to determine whether the chosen measurements can distinguish the parameters. Do not treat a fitted parameter as uniquely identified without evidence.

### WP4 — Experimental comparison and model calibration (conditional)
If equipment, access and safety procedures are available, conduct or use existing modal / frequency-response measurements. Compare frequency, amplitude, phase and mode-shape information as appropriate. Estimate damping using a suitable model-fitting or modal-identification method; do not equate the FRF imaginary component directly with damping. Report measurement uncertainty and model discrepancy.

### WP5 — Thesis and dissemination
Consolidate the model, assumptions, verification, sensitivity results and limitations into a master's thesis. Publication is a possible outcome, not a guaranteed deliverable.

## 5. Expected contribution
The intended contribution is a transparent workflow for understanding how support and housing assumptions affect spindle-system dynamic predictions, and for deciding which parameters can be meaningfully calibrated from available evidence. The work is not premised on a predetermined percentage improvement or a guaranteed real-time control outcome.
